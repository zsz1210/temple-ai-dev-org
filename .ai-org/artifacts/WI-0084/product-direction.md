# Product Direction — Public-Alpha release truth

## Reader problem

Temple's older roadmap described completed phases and long-range ambitions, but no longer made the next decision obvious. A reader could mistake a large implemented surface for production readiness, while a maintainer could mistake old non-terminal Work Items for current delivery scope. The release conversation also mixed three separate questions: whether the local framework works, whether it is safe to publish an Alpha, and whether it is qualified for production or enterprise use.

## Intended outcome

The roadmap should let a reader answer, in order:

1. What already works?
2. What must be true before the first public Alpha?
3. What evidence should be collected from real adoption after that release?
4. What remains outside a local Alpha claim?

The release-readiness document should give maintainers a durable checklist for one exact candidate revision. The license brief should help the Human Principal choose without presenting the recommendation as an approved migration.

## Product rules

1. Work Item state follows canonical files and exact evidence, not chat history or Dashboard appearance.
2. A historical passing revision is evidence history, not proof for a later release candidate.
3. Failed and blocked experiments remain visible when their limitations are still relevant.
4. English, Japanese, and Traditional Chinese roadmaps carry the same facts and hierarchy, but each edition is written as natural technical prose for that language.
5. Roadmap promises stop at verified capability. Real-world, production, enterprise, external-write, and automatic-routing claims remain later until separately evidenced.
6. A test-readiness item is marked verified, pending, partial, or blocked; absence of evidence is never rewritten as success.
7. MIT versus Apache-2.0 is a governance choice. Documentation may recommend; only the Human Principal authorizes a repository license change.

## Acceptance boundary

This Work Item succeeds when canonical stale items have evidence-backed dispositions, the three roadmaps describe the same current release stage, the remaining Alpha tests are explicitly classified, and the license trade-off is recorded. It does not need to fix every identified release blocker or perform a release.
